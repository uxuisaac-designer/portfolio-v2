Kick Game sells rare and collectible sneakers and streetwear — seven stores in
the UK, shipping globally. I was brought in to improve the buying experience
across the site.

## Two kinds of buyer

Interviews and a customer survey surfaced the same friction from two
directions. Some buyers arrive knowing exactly what they want: a specific
model, a specific size, often something they’ve been hunting for months. Others
arrive to see what’s landed.

The site served neither well. Search returned poor results for the first group.
Navigation was unintuitive for the second. Both were failing at the same job —
helping someone find a pair — from opposite ends.

## What the business wanted

I ran a focus group with the founders and team. Their priorities were
different: the product display page and the checkout. Where the two inputs met
was the interesting part. Customers couldn’t find things; the business couldn’t
convert the people who did. Same funnel, different halves.

That gave the work two problems rather than six features.

## Finding things

Search and navigation together. I rebuilt the mega menu around how people
actually browse — streamlined categories, clearer priority, visual cues for the
sections that mattered most.

For search, autocomplete suggestions, richer product detail in the results, and
a “Hot right now” surface for the browsing group. The specific-pair hunters got
precision; the browsers got a reason to keep looking.

## Deciding and buying

The product display page carried the most weight. Higher-quality imagery,
tighter descriptions, and the ability to see alternative colourways without
leaving the page — the single change that most affected how long people stayed.

<Figure
  src="/placeholder.png"
  caption="The product display page, with alternative colourways inline."
/>

Then the size selector, which was the clearest source of hesitation in
interviews, and the checkout flow behind it. Fewer steps, clearer state.

<Figure
  src="/placeholder.png"
  caption="The size selector — the clearest source of hesitation in interviews."
/>

Collection pages were rebuilt alongside: better filtering, clearer
categorisation, a more consistent grid.

## What I’d do differently

Search and navigation shipped first, with the PDP and checkout work following
over the next quarter. The complaint that started the project — customers
unable to find pairs they knew Kick Game stocked — stopped coming up.

I don’t have the post-launch numbers. If I ran this again I’d instrument
zero-result searches, add-to-cart rate and cart-to-purchase before shipping
anything. The changes were right and the reasoning held, but I couldn’t prove
it, and that’s the part I’d fix.
