Listing a car on Car & Classic is free, and that is what sets it apart. The
business needed another source of revenue that left the free listing whole.

Boost lets a seller pay to put their listing above other people’s in search. I
designed it, working with a product manager and our developers, and it shipped
in early 2025 on web, the app and the dealer platform. Around 15% of listings
are now boosted.

## Every car felt equal

Search had no hierarchy beyond what the buyer typed. Search for a BMW M3 and
every M3 came back on the same footing, however much its seller wanted it
found. It was a car park with no front row.

Several revenue streams were in the pipeline, and the C-suite chose this one.
Paying for position suits a marketplace where listing costs nothing. The free
route stays as it was, and a seller who wants more can buy it.

## Pricing a place

We looked at how other car marketplaces sell promotion — Auto Trader,
PistonHeads and Collecting Cars — and at sites outside cars that promote
people’s adverts, including eBay, Depop and Vinted.

To set the price, we asked a handful of sellers we had worked with before how
much they would pay to put their listing above everyone else’s.

## Two tiers, not three

There are two paid tiers above Basic. Featured lifts a listing above Basic
adverts in search. Spotlight puts it above Featured and Basic both. At launch
they cost £29.99 and £69.99.

Buyers see the difference on the listing card, which carries a Featured or
Spotlight tag.

During discovery we also considered a third tier, Spotlight Pro, at a much
higher price. We cut it as unnecessary. The paid extra that took its place was
professional photography of the seller’s car.

## Offered last

The choice comes towards the end of the listing flow, once the seller has
entered everything about their car. By then the details are done, and a boost
is an add-on to a finished listing rather than one more decision in the middle
of them.

Basic stays selected by default. The paid tiers have to win the seller over on
what they offer, and a seller who passes can boost later from their account.

## What sellers chose

A paid step inside a free flow could have cost free listings. It barely did:
listing completion fell by less than 2% in the first week, then returned to
normal.

Around 15% of listings are now boosted. We estimate boosted cars sell three to
five times faster than basic ones, varying by vehicle, since demand differs
from one kind of car to another.

## Priced by the crowd

After launch we moved to dynamic pricing. One price everywhere treated every
search the same, and they are not. Rising above a crowd of listings is worth
more than rising above a few, and charging a seller whose car has little
competition the same as one whose car has plenty did not seem fair. The front row costs more when the car
park is full.

Spotlight still costs more than Featured, but both now move with the listing.
One seller might see Featured at £34.99 and Spotlight at £49.99. Another sees
£19.99 and £29.99.

## What I’d do differently

I designed Boost from the seller’s side, and buyers got a tag. Next time I
would design for the buyer as deliberately: how boosted listings sit among the
rest of the results, and how a paid position reads to someone searching. A
buyer who stops trusting the order of results stops being worth ranking above.

I would measure that alongside the sales: enquiries on Basic listings in
results that carry boosted ones, and whether buyers scroll past the top of the
page.
