The contents list beside a case study marks the section you are reading with
a 6px square. When the section changes, the square travels to the new label.

The labels are ragged and the square sits after the text, so every move is
diagonal. It travels a quadratic curve that bows right, toward the content
column, by 12px on a one-step move and up to 32px on a longer one, instead
of cutting back through the words. It turns a quarter clockwise on the way.
A square looks the same at both ends of a quarter turn, so the turn is only
ever seen in flight.

Scrolling interrupts it constantly, and that shaped the rest. The first
version added 90° to wherever the angle had got to, and a move cut short at
45° left the square resting as a diamond from then on. Now the angle always
lands on the next multiple of 90. Progress runs on a critically damped
spring rather than a fixed duration, so an interruption bends the path
instead of restarting it from a standstill. Carried velocity is clamped
below the square root of the stiffness, which guarantees no overshoot rather
than making it unlikely.

Half the path is covered in 133ms and 90% of it by 317ms. Clicking a label
holds the observer for 700ms, so a smooth scroll past four headings is one
move rather than five.
