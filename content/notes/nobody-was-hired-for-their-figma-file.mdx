Before I was a designer I spent over a year as a design recruiter. That is the
side of the table where portfolios get opened quickly, skimmed, and passed
around to people who are not designers — and where you hear what gets said
about them afterwards.

Almost none of what was said was about the work.

## What the panel is reading

The work is on the page, and the work is not mostly what is being assessed.
Three things are.

Personality, which sounds like a soft thing to screen for until you watch a
panel choose between two candidates of equal ability. Problem solving, which is
the one everybody expects. Then whether you can take something complicated and
make it easy for somebody else to understand.

The third is different from the other two, because a portfolio does not
describe it. It performs it. A case study about a genuinely messy problem,
written so that an engineer or a founder can follow it, is that skill happening
in front of the reader. The same case study written so that only another
designer can follow it is also that skill happening in front of the reader, in
the other direction. There is no way to claim it in a sentence and no way to
avoid demonstrating it.

That is the proxy. The file is not being marked. The file is evidence about how
you think and how well you can carry someone else through it.

## Mine worked on the wrong people

I had a portfolio that designers liked. Other designers told me so, and I
believed them, because they were the people whose opinion I had gone looking
for.

It did not work on hiring managers. It did not work on anyone in the process
who was not a designer — the engineer on the panel, the founder, whoever
actually owned the budget. The things it was doing well were things you needed
a design background to notice, and the things it was doing badly were the
things everyone else needed.

Designers were not the ones deciding. They were the ones giving compliments.

## The advice builds the wrong thing

Most portfolio advice is written by designers, read by designers, and optimised
for a designer audience, which is the one audience that does not make the
decision. Stand out. Show craft. Make it memorable.

Follow enough of that and you get an over-designed portfolio: a site whose
layout is doing so much arguing on its own behalf that the work has to compete
with it. Every one of those choices is defensible in isolation. Together they
produce a thing that is harder to read than the problem it is describing, which
is the exact inversion of the skill it was built to demonstrate.

## The interview is a research session

The part of an interview that gets treated as small talk is the part I paid
most attention to.

Someone who asks real questions — about the users, the constraints, why the
company does the odd thing it does — is showing you how they behave in a
research session. Curiosity is difficult to fake for an hour, and getting a
stranger to tell you something true is most of the job. A candidate who is
genuinely interested in the people on the other side of the table will be
genuinely interested in the people on the other side of the product.

You cannot put that in a Figma file. It is a large part of what the file is
standing in for.

## What this one is for

Two reasons, and the first is the obvious one: somewhere to put the work and
the thinking, in a format that does not require a design background to get
through. Everything here is a single column of text you can read on a phone.
That is a position, not a shortage of ideas.

The second reason is less respectable and more honest. This is somewhere to put
the design work I do not get to do. At a company there are brand guidelines, a
roadmap, stakeholders, and a dozen reasonable people with opinions that are
individually sensible — the fanned thumbnails, the greeting that types itself
in eight languages, the underline that wipes rather than appears. None of those
would survive a review, and none of them needed to.

Nobody signed this page off. There was no guideline to check it against and no
one to talk me out of it. That is most of why it exists.
