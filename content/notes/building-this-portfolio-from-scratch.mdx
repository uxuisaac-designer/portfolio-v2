Ninety-four commits in, the thing that has cost me the most time is a square
six pixels wide.

It sits in the sidebar of a case study, marking the heading you are currently
reading. It travels about a centimetre. It went through three written plans,
and the third one deliberately threw away the first.

A case study would cut that. A case study has a job — here was the problem,
here is what I changed, here is the number it moved — and a square that travels
a centimetre has nowhere to sit in it. So this is the other version — what
the building was actually like, which most portfolios skip.

## Three plans for a six-pixel square

The first plan was about the curve. The marker travelled on
cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1), a strong ease-out — the right family for
something arriving or leaving, the wrong one for something moving between two
places you can already see. At the 400ms it had been given, that curve puts 90%
of the travel in the first 147ms and spends the last 112ms covering under 1% of
the distance. The square darted, then sat still while the frame loop kept
running. It read as a stutter rather than a move.

That fix was two lines, and it worked.

The second plan was a different problem wearing the same clothes. Clicking a
heading smooth-scrolls the page, and the scroll spy fires on every heading it
passes on the way down, so a click four headings away was five separate moves
stacked on top of each other. The marker chased the scroll instead of going
where I had sent it. Now a click owns the active section for 700ms and the
observer stands down until the page settles.

The third plan deleted the first.

It replaced the fixed tween with a critically damped spring — stiffness 157.9,
damping 25.13 — so an interrupted move bends instead of restarting from a
standing start. Carried velocity is rescaled by the ratio of the old path to
the new, then clamped below the square root of the stiffness, which is what
makes overshoot impossible rather than merely unlikely. Half the path is
covered in 133ms and 90% by 317ms. The rest is a settle you cannot see.

Landing it discarded the curve the first plan had spent its whole length
tuning. That was written down in advance: the first plan was the cheap
experiment that would tell me whether the third was worth starting at all.
Knowing in advance that work will be thrown away is a different thing from
wasting it, and I would rather write the two-line version first every time.

## Tabs, and then not tabs

The second-longest decision was the row of three words under the intro.

Tabs were the obvious answer and the wrong one. Tabs imply panels inside a
page. These are routes — Index, Notes, Lab — and the browser already has a
back button that expects them to behave like routes. So it became a segmented
control, hand-built, shrunk to the width of its own labels, sitting in the flow
rather than pinned across the top like chrome.

The part that took the time was a colour. The track sits one step off the
background and the active pill is the background, so the selection reads as cut
out of the track rather than laid on top of it. My first attempt used the hover
colour for the track. On a desktop monitor it looked fine. Measured, the pill
against that track came out at 1.08:1, which on a phone in daylight is not a
difference at all — the control simply had no visible selection. Moving the
track to the border colour fixed it.

I would not have caught that by looking. I caught it by measuring, after the
thing had already looked finished for two days.

## Two lines, one of them deliberately too faint

Every link on this site is two lines rather than one. A resting line that never
moves, and a second line in the text colour that wipes in from the left on
hover and, on the way out, keeps going and leaves by the right edge instead of
retracting the way it came.

That exit is the detail I am fondest of and the one nobody will ever notice.
The transform origin flips between left and right, and it is deliberately left
out of the transition list so the flip happens instantly — which is safe only
because it can only ever happen while the line is at full width or no width,
where both origins draw exactly the same box. Nothing jumps because there is
nothing to jump.

Then the resting line. It sits at oklch(0.750 0.003 58), which measures 2.1:1
against the background. The floor for a non-text element is 3:1, so it is under
it, and I shipped it anyway.

The reason is that the gap between the two lines is the animation. Lift the
resting line towards the text colour and the second line stops registering as
movement, because there is less and less left to travel. I wanted the distance
between resting and hovered to be obvious, and the price of that distance is a
resting line fainter than the guideline allows. Every link on the page carries
it, so the shortfall is real rather than decorative — there is no second signal
doing the job while the colour falls short.

It sits right with me. It is also written down in the project file as an open
item, with the value that would clear the threshold unaided — oklch(0.640) —
sitting next to it, so the day I add a second affordance to links I know
exactly what to change.

## What I borrowed, and what I handed back

Five sites were open in tabs the whole time: emilkowal.ski, jakub.kr,
benji.org, shedsgns.me, mikes.cv. Single column, monochrome, list-based rows,
no navigation to speak of.

What I took was the restraint. What I handed back was the extremity of it. Some
of those sites are quieter than I wanted to be — I needed a little more going
on than a list of links and a name. Others had gone the other way and built
light and dark mode experiences elaborate enough that switching became an
event. I wanted the theme to be a fact about the page, not a feature of it.

So the borrowing was never wholesale. There is exactly one piece of colour on
this entire site, a flag in the footer, and it is there because a monochrome
page with one deliberate exception says more than a monochrome page.

## Where the tools helped

AI ran through this build as a tool, in three places. Structuring — turning a
pile of intentions into an order of work, which is most of what those animation
plans in `plans/` are. Coding. Arguing with me about a handful of the design
decisions, including two of the three above.

The 147ms figure earlier is a fair example. Left alone I would have eyeballed
that curve, called it fine, and moved on with the stutter still in it.

## What is still wrong with it

Six things, and they are written down.

Three of the project rows still show placeholder images, which in dark mode
means three white rectangles glowing on a dark background. The nav segments are
buttons rather than links, so you cannot middle-click them, open them in a new
tab, or crawl them. The greeting cycles eight languages and three of them fall
back to system fonts, because the typeface I chose has no CJK or Arabic glyphs
and I am not loading three more font families for three words. Six of the seven
project rows are not links yet, because the case studies behind them are not
written. The resting underline is under contrast. Route changes are a CSS
fade rather than the View Transitions API, because reaching React’s version of
it currently means depending on a packaging detail that would break at runtime
rather than at build.

None of those is a bug I have not noticed. Each one has a line in `CLAUDE.md`
saying what it costs and what would unblock it, which is the only reason I can
list them from memory. The site is not finished. The list of what is unfinished
is.
