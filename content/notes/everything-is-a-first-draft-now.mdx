I do not sketch any more. Not less — I have stopped.

I used to draw a component’s states by hand. Loading, empty, error, the one
where somebody’s name is too long. Three or four versions, all of them rough,
until I could see which one was right. That is gone from my week now. I
generate eight in a few minutes instead, and the same thing has happened to the
research work around it — the guide I write before a session, the first pass at
what came out of one.

My week did not get shorter.

## The bottleneck was never the drawing

AI did not remove my bottleneck. It made it visible.

Sketching was never the slow part. Deciding was. I could always draw faster
than I could choose, and the drawing gave me somewhere to put my hands while
the choosing went on underneath it. Take the drawing away and the choosing is
left standing on its own, with nothing in front of it to hide behind.

So I produce eight directions in the time two used to take, and I am still the
one person standing between those eight and a decision. The queue got longer.
The gate is exactly as wide as it was.

That is why "it makes you faster" lands oddly on me. Faster at what? The part
that was never expensive got cheaper. The part that was always expensive is
sitting exactly where it was, and it now has twice as much arriving at it.

There is a version of this where more options is straightforwardly good, and I
have had days like that — eight directions where the third one is obviously
right and I would never have drawn it myself. There is another version where
eight directions is eight ways to not decide yet, and I have had more of those
than I would like. The difference between the two days is not the tool.

## One-shotting is rare

The eight are not eight answers. They are eight starting points, and the wrong
ones are wrong in ways that take longer to spot than they took to produce.

It is rare to get a thing in one. Most of what I do now is tweak and check,
tweak and check, and that is the work — it is also most of the fun of it, which
I did not expect to still be true. Refinement is a skill. It did not stop being
one when the first draft got free.

That is the change, and it is what the title of this note means. A first draft
used to be a thing you made. Now it is the state everything shows up in, and
the whole of the job is what you do to it after it arrives. The generating is
not the craft. The checking is.

## This site is the argument, in both directions

For: this site exists, and it is at the standard I wanted rather than the
standard I could afford. Making the artefact cheap is what made the writing
affordable. Every hour that did not go into hand-building a segmented control
went somewhere better, and the case studies are longer and more honest for it.

Against, partly: the parts of this site I am proudest of are the parts I argued
hardest about. The contents marker took three written plans and I fought for
every one of them. The link underline sits under the contrast floor because I
decided the wipe mattered more, and I can still tell you exactly why. Those are
mine.

The parts I let run look fine and mean nothing to me. I could not defend them
in a room. If someone asked me why a particular spacing or a particular
transition is the way it is, I would find myself reconstructing a reason rather
than remembering one.

That is the same argument working in reverse. The artefact got cheap, and the
things I care about are precisely the ones where I refused to let it stay
cheap.

## The ladder

I spent my first two years producing things badly until I got good at them.
That is how it worked. You did the unglamorous version of the task enough times
that your hands learned something your head could not be told.

That ladder is being removed, and I do not know what replaces it. The obvious
answer is that judgement gets taught directly, and I am not convinced judgement
can be taught directly — mine came from producing a lot of bad work and being
in the room when someone explained why it was bad.

I would still hire a junior. I would spend that time on their judgement and
none of it on their craft.
